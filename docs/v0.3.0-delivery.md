# v0.3.0: what was planned, what shipped, and what is wrong with it

This file records what v0.3.0 delivers and what is wrong with it, so that
reading the code and reading this do not leave different impressions of what
exists. `docs/scope.md` says what the project does not attempt; no correlated
method is implemented, and no partial one is in the tree.

Everything named here as delivered has a test that fails if it stops working.
Everything named as a known problem is a problem in the released code, not a
hypothetical one.

## Delivered

### Licensing

v0.2.4 was not compliant, in four separate ways, and all four are fixed:

- no upstream copyright notice was retained anywhere, though every OpenMOPAC
  source file carries one and Apache-2.0 4(c) requires it;
- no modification notice or date, required by Apache-2.0 4(b) and GPL-3.0 5(a);
- `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` said it recorded "parameter-data sources" and that no
  upstream source was redistributed, while about a dozen modules are line-level
  ports of Apache-2.0 Fortran;
- `src/data/element_data.csv` was embedded and attributed nowhere.

New: `NOTICE`, `LICENSES/` (ten texts), `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES_RUST.md` (87 linked
and 25 build-time crates), `src/licenses.rs` with everything embedded and
printable through `xndo_rs_cli licenses`, `tools/collect_rust_licenses.py`,
`tools/privacy_scan.py`, and 16 tests in `tests/attribution.rs`.

The collector reads the files each crate ships rather than trusting its SPDX
field, because `faer` declares `MIT` and ships `COPYING.EIGEN.MPL2`. A generator
that trusts the field drops the MPL-2.0 obligation entirely, and `faer` is a
direct dependency of every artifact this project produces.

### Oracle verification

All six methods, against the program each one's parameters came from:

| method | oracle | molecules | elements | independent points |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| MNDO | OpenMOPAC 23.2.5 | 51 | 19 | 309 |
| MNDO/d | OpenMOPAC 23.2.5 | 37 | 17 | 230 |
| ZINDO/S | OpenMOPAC 23.2.5 `INDO` | 41 | 10 | 465 |
| ZINDO/S CIS | OpenMOPAC 23.2.5 `INDO CIS` | 24 | 10 | 322 |
| CNDO/2 | MolDS 0.3.1 | 17 | 6 | 270 |
| INDO | MolDS 0.3.1 | 14 | 5 | 208 |
| MINDO/3 | MOPAC7 1.15 | 36 | 10 | 611 |

2415 independent points. Every suite is enforced, every tolerance floor is
committed with the measured worst case beside it, and the list of known
disagreements is **empty**.

`tests/data/ORACLE_NOTES.md` is the long version: 26 numbered items covering
what each oracle can and cannot do, and every defect the comparison found.

### Orbital energies and HOMO/LUMO

`src/orbitals.rs` gives one shape all four engines convert to, exposed through
`CalculationResult::orbitals()` and `run_orbitals()` in Rust,
`orbital_energies()` in Python, `XNDO.get_orbital_energies()` in ASE, and an
`orbitals` subcommand in the CLI.

### Molden wavefunction output

`src/sto.rs` (STO-nG expansions), `src/gto.rs` (Gaussian overlap integrals) and
`src/molden.rs`. Coefficients are back-transformed with `S^(-1/2)`, because the
engines assume an orthonormal AO basis and a Molden file does not describe one.
`molden_string`/`write_molden` in Rust, `molden()` in Python,
`XNDO.write_molden()` in ASE, and both a `molden` subcommand and a `--molden`
flag in the CLI.

### Photoexcitation study

`studies/photoexcitation/`, run as a test. Its headline result: ZINDO/S keeps
the exact `1/R` charge-transfer asymptote and gets its coefficient 20% too
large, recovered as 1.1988 against the parameterisation's `TOMK = 1.2` from
excitation energies alone.

## Known problems

### Defects found and fixed in this release

Listed because they were present in v0.2.4 and anyone comparing results across
versions needs to know the numbers moved:

- **`hp2_ev` was stored rounded** for N, Si, S and Cl, 0.005 eV each. MOPAC7
  computes that integral as `(gpp - gp2)/2` and stores nothing, so the relation
  is structural. Worth 2.2e-2 eV per chlorine atom; CCl4 was 8.6e-2 eV out.
- **MINDO/3's Slater exponents were not restated in MOPAC7's Bohr**, a 1.93e-5
  relative error in every overlap exponential, worth a few times 1e-4 eV per
  atom pair. Benzene was 4.5e-3 eV out.
- **The SCF spin guess put the excess spin where the density already was**,
  which is backwards. Planar methyl under MINDO/3 converged to a real,
  aufbau-satisfying SCF solution **3.07 eV above** the right one, with the
  unpaired electron in a C-H antibonding orbital and zero spin population on the
  carbon `p_z` where MOPAC7 has exactly 1.0. Every accelerator and damping
  factor reached the same wrong answer.
- **The CPHF solvers reported nothing when they ran out of iterations.** All
  three returned their last iterate after 100 cycles with no indication it was
  not converged. Water does not fit in 100: it reaches 1.88e-8 against a 1e-9
  threshold. So every Hessian this crate produced, in every version up to and
  including v0.2.4, was built from a response that had not met its own stated
  tolerance. The finite-difference tests passed anyway, because 2e-8 is a small
  error, which is precisely why nothing caught it. The limit is now 500 and
  falling off the end is an error.
- **The ZINDO/S UHF path had no SCF accelerator at all**, the one place the
  documented A-DIIS/CDIIS default did not apply.
- **The Python package did not import.** `orbital_energies`, `molden` and
  `third_party_licenses` were registered in the compiled `_native` module and
  re-exported by `xndo_rs/__init__.py`, but the `native.py` shim between them
  defined none of the three, so `import xndo_rs` raised `AttributeError` on its
  sixth line for anyone who installed the wheel. The ASE calculator's
  `get_orbital_energies` and `write_molden` called the same two missing names.
  This was introduced with those features in this cycle and never reached a
  release, but it is listed because it is the defect that says most about the
  test suite: the Rust side was covered, the documentation was correct, and the
  Python surface test asserted a hand-written list of names that nobody had
  extended. `tests/test_python_api.py` now derives the expected set from
  `_native` and asserts all three layers agree, so the next unwired function
  fails there rather than on a user's first import.
- **Release binaries carried the build machine's home directory.** A release
  build of the CLI and of `_native.pyd` embedded the project path, the cargo
  registry path and the bare account name -- several hundred occurrences each --
  because nothing stripped symbols and nothing remapped the prefixes. Found by
  running `tools/privacy_scan.py --artifacts` against the built wheel rather
  than against the source tree, which is the distinction that matters: the tree
  was clean the whole time. `[profile.release]` now sets `strip = "symbols"`
  and README.md carries the three `--remap-path-prefix` flags that clear the
  rest; with both, the scan goes to zero findings.
- **The repository stored a line-ending-converted copy of OpenMOPAC's
  `LICENSE`.** `third_party/mopac/LICENSE` is meant to be a verbatim copy, and
  `tests/attribution.rs` pins its SHA-256 to upstream's. The working tree
  matched; the committed blob did not, because the file was added before
  `.gitattributes` set `* -text` and was converted to LF on the way in. So the
  test passed in this working tree and failed for anyone who cloned or unpacked
  the project -- which is the worst arrangement, since the copy that is wrong is
  the copy that gets distributed. `examples/bench102.xyz` had the same problem.
  Both blobs have been renormalised, and all 153 tracked files now match their
  working-tree bytes exactly. Found by extracting the release archive into a
  clean directory and running the suite from there, which is the only check that
  looks at what is actually shipped.

### Limitations in the released code

These are not going to be fixed in v0.3.0 and are stated so that nobody
discovers them by getting a wrong answer:

- **MINDO/3 has no dipole moment.** It is the only engine without one, so
  `single_point` returns no `dipole_debye` for it and the MINDO/3 oracle suite
  compares no dipole. The reference file carries MOPAC7's values and the
  comparison is a one-line change once the engine can produce one.
- **ZINDO/S has no d branch.** Elements needing one are rejected, in the ground
  state and in Molden output alike, rather than being run without their d
  functions.
- **CNDO/2 and INDO dipoles are not comparable with MolDS.** MolDS contracts the
  density with position integrals over Gaussian-expanded Slater AOs including
  two-centre terms; this crate uses the NDDO form (atom-centred point charges
  plus the one-centre s-p hybrid term), which is what MOPAC and the ZINDO/S
  engine here use. On CH4 the two differ by 0.23 D. Neither is wrong; they are
  different definitions, and the suites compare everything except the dipole.
- **MINDO/3's pair table covers 40 of the 55 pairs** its ten elements could
  form. F-S is one of the missing fifteen, so SF6 cannot be run. That is the
  published model's limit, not this implementation's.
- **Molden for CNDO/2 and INDO uses one principal quantum number per atom** for
  every shell, because that is how those engines define their basis. The file
  says so in its own title.
- **The photoexcitation study benchmarks nothing.** It measures the model
  against exactly known limits -- an asymptotic form, a reflection symmetry, a
  variational bound -- and deliberately makes no accuracy claim, because the
  reference data for one is not in this repository and post-HF, which would be
  needed to separate the CIS ansatz's error from the Hamiltonian's, is out of
  scope. Its own report says this.

### Residuals that are the oracle's, not this crate's

- MNDO germane (1.209e-3 kcal/mol) and MNDO/d H2S (9.014e-4) sit at the branch
  point of MOPAC's truncated B-integral series. Predicted and confirmed by
  locating the crossing and watching the disagreement step across it.
- CNDO/2 and INDO energies floor at about 4e-4 eV because MolDS prints seven
  significant figures and no keyword widens the format.
- MINDO/3 floors at MOPAC7's four-decimal charges and eigenvalues.

## Still open from the plan

Named rather than quietly dropped:

- `docs/licensing.md`, an obligation-to-document map, was planned and not
  written. The information is in `NOTICE` and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`; what is
  missing is the index.
- `.github/workflows/ci.yml` was planned and not written. Every check it would
  run exists and is runnable (`cargo test`, `cargo clippy`, the three
  `tools/validate_*.py`, `tools/privacy_scan.py --tree .`,
  `tools/collect_rust_licenses.py --check`,
  `tools/oracle/build_reference_set.py --check`,
  `tools/study/render_report.py --check`), but nothing runs them automatically.
- `tools/validate_cndo_indo_wiring.py:58` still asserts a numeric literal that
  the plan identified as brittle.
